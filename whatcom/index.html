How this works, and what it does not know

Wind: the HRRR and GFS forecasts (Open-Meteo) at three points along the lake, blended by distance; the wind arrow shows the middle point. National Weather Service alerts for the lake, and Bellingham airport's latest observation for comparison. Lakeside home weather stations (CWOP) for what the lake did earlier today; the National Weather Service passes them on about four hours late. Floathaven Airstrip (WA90) has no weather instruments, so there is nothing to read there. For each spot on the lake and each wind direction the page knows how much open water lies upwind (the fetch, past the islands and points), how much the forested shore and the hills just upwind take out of the wind, and so how big the wind-blown chop grows (fetch-limited wave growth). Chop is the significant wave height: the bigger third of the waves.

Guide values for shells: under 10 cm smooth; 10 to 20 cm workable; 20 to 30 cm rough, experienced crews and bigger boats; over 30 cm or gusts over 30 mph, stay off in small boats. They are starting points, not rules: you know your boats and your crews.

Not known: there is no live wind sensor on the lake, so the hills' channelling of the wind along the valley is only as good as the 3 km forecast; the shelter of the hills is an estimate. Water temperature is not published live: from late autumn to spring the lake is cold enough to be the bigger risk, and there is no rescue service on the lake. Routes are traced from screenshots and approximate.
