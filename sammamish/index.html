How this works, and what it does not know

Wind: the HRRR and GFS forecasts (Open-Meteo) at three points along the lake, blended by distance; the wind arrow shows the middle point. National Weather Service alerts for the lake, and the nearest airport's latest observation for comparison. Lakeside home weather stations (Citizen Weather Observer Program), relayed every 15 minutes; when they show the lake running windier or calmer than forecast, the next three hours are adjusted (calibrated on two months of their history). Floathaven Airstrip (WA90) has no weather instruments, so there is nothing to read there. For each spot on the lake and each wind direction the page knows how much open water lies upwind (the fetch, past the islands and points), how much the forested shore and the hills just upwind take out of the wind, and so how big the wind-blown chop grows (fetch-limited wave growth). Chop is the significant wave height: the bigger third of the waves.

Guide values for shells: under 10 cm smooth; 10 to 20 cm workable; 20 to 30 cm rough, experienced crews and bigger boats; over 30 cm or gusts over 30 mph, stay off in small boats. They are starting points, not rules: you know your boats and your crews.

Not known: there is no live wind sensor on the lake, so the hills' channelling of the wind along the valley is only as good as the 3 km forecast; the shelter of the hills is an estimate. Fog: see its own note above. Where the lake has a buoy that reports the water temperature, the fog outlook uses it; from late autumn to spring the water is cold enough to be the bigger risk. Routes are approximate.
